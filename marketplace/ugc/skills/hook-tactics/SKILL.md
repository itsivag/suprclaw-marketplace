# Hook Tactics Skill

This skill is a strategic reference library. It answers two questions:
1. **What is this tactic?** (definition + example)
2. **When should I use it?** (deployment guidance)

For *how to write* the hook once the tactic is selected, refer to the **Hook Writing Skill**.

---

## How Tactics Relate to Psychological Triggers

- **Tactics** = the strategic frame or format of the hook (the what)
- **Psychological triggers** = the emotional mechanism that makes it work (the how)

Every tactic is executed through one or more psychological triggers.

## The 35 Tactic Definitions

### Aspirational
Frames the identity, lifestyle, or status the viewer wants. Speaks to who they want to become.
**Best for:** Unaware, Problem-Aware. Lifestyle, beauty, fitness products.
**Trigger:** Aspiration/Desire
*Example: "Glow like never before."*

### Authority
Establishes credibility via expertise, credentials, certifications.
**Best for:** Health, wellness, supplements, skincare. Skeptical audiences.
**Trigger:** Social Proof/Credibility
*Example: "Dermatologist recommended."*

### Belief
Opens with the brand's point of view, mission, or values.
**Best for:** Brand-building, mission-driven products.
**Trigger:** Aspiration/Desire, Contrarian
*Example: "We believe skincare should be simple."*

### Bold Claim
Makes an outsized, extreme, or superlative promise.
**Best for:** Competitive categories where differentiation is hard.
**Trigger:** Pattern Interrupt, Urgency/Stakes
*Example: "The world's best..."*

### Call To Action First
Opens with an explicit shopping or action instruction.
**Best for:** Most-Aware audiences. Retargeting. Sale campaigns.
**Trigger:** Urgency/Stakes
*Example: "Shop now." / "Try it today."*

### Challenge
Competitive framing that invites the viewer to test, attempt, or prove something.
**Best for:** Fitness, gaming, performance categories.
**Trigger:** Identity Call-Out, Pattern Interrupt
*Example: "I bet you can't finish this."*

### Confession
A candid, honest admission that builds credibility through vulnerability.
**Best for:** Rebuilding trust, countering skepticism.
**Trigger:** Social Proof/Credibility, Pattern Interrupt
*Example: "I was wrong about sunscreen."*

### Contrast
Juxtaposes two things to highlight a mismatch or clear superiority.
**Best for:** Price-sensitive audiences, upgrade messaging.
**Trigger:** Pattern Interrupt, Pain Agitation
*Example: "Don't put $5 gas in a $50,000 car."*

### Contrarian
Deliberately goes against conventional wisdom or expected advice.
**Best for:** Educated, skeptical audiences. Categories full of conventional advice.
**Trigger:** Pattern Interrupt, Contrarian/Myth-Busting
*Example: "I'm a vet who doesn't take her dog to the vet."*

### Curiosity
Creates an open loop or tease that the viewer needs to close.
**Best for:** Top of funnel, content-led ads.
**Trigger:** Curiosity Gap
*Example: "Water doesn't hydrate you."*

### Demographic Callout
Names a specific audience segment directly to qualify relevance.
**Best for:** Niche products, highly segmented audiences.
**Trigger:** Identity Call-Out
*Example: "For runners with bad knees."*

### Direct Address
Speaks directly and personally to the viewer. Creates immediate intimacy.
**Best for:** Social video where breaking the fourth wall creates a pattern interrupt.
**Trigger:** Identity Call-Out, Pattern Interrupt
*Example: "Hey you..." / "Stop scrolling..."*

### Directive
An imperative that instructs the viewer to change a behavior, habit, or mindset.
**Best for:** Problem-Aware and Solution-Aware. Health, finance, self-improvement.
**Trigger:** Pattern Interrupt, Pain Agitation
*Example: "Stop wasting money on bras."*

### Exclusivity
Signals that access is selective, limited, or not for everyone.
**Best for:** Premium, luxury products. High-ticket offers.
**Trigger:** Urgency/Stakes, Identity Call-Out
*Example: "By invite only."*

### Explainer
Explains the reason behind something using "why" framing.
**Best for:** Problem-Aware audiences. Science-backed products.
**Trigger:** Curiosity Gap, Contrarian/Myth-Busting
*Example: "Why your towels stay musty."*

### FOMO
Creates anxiety about missing out on a trend or social moment.
**Best for:** Product-Aware. Viral or trend-driven products.
**Trigger:** Urgency/Stakes, Social Proof/Credibility
*Example: "Everyone's switching to this..."*

### How To
An instructional promise that teaches the viewer how to accomplish something.
**Best for:** Problem-Aware and Solution-Aware audiences.
**Trigger:** Curiosity Gap, Pain Agitation
*Example: "How to fix dry skin fast."*

### If Then
Qualifies the viewer with a condition, then delivers a promise or action.
**Best for:** Segmented audiences with a clear, nameable condition.
**Trigger:** Identity Call-Out, Pain Agitation
*Example: "If you have oily skin, do this."*

### Listicle
Numbered or list-based framing that organizes information into digestible items.
**Best for:** Content-heavy campaigns, educational top-of-funnel.
**Trigger:** Curiosity Gap, Social Proof/Credibility
*Example: "5 steps to clearer skin."*

### Myth Busting
Directly debunks a widely held misconception.
**Best for:** Sophisticated, educated audiences. Science-backed brands.
**Trigger:** Pattern Interrupt, Contrarian/Myth-Busting
*Example: "SPF 100 doesn't block 100%."*

### Offer Only
Uses a discount or monetary incentive as the sole hook.
**Best for:** Most-Aware audiences. Sale events.
**Trigger:** Urgency/Stakes
*Example: "$500 Off." / "20% Discount."*

### Price Anchor
Frames the cost against a familiar, relatable benchmark.
**Best for:** Mid-to-high ticket products. Price-sensitive audiences.
**Trigger:** Pattern Interrupt, Pain Agitation
*Example: "Less than your daily coffee."*

### Question
Opens with a posed problem, challenge, or curiosity gap in question form.
**Best for:** All awareness stages. Effective at Problem-Aware.
**Trigger:** Curiosity Gap, Pain Agitation
*Example: "Struggling with X?" / "Did you know...?"*

### Reasons Why
Opens with a specific number + "reasons why" phrasing.
**Best for:** Solution-Aware and Product-Aware audiences.
**Trigger:** Social Proof/Credibility, Curiosity Gap
*Example: "3 reasons why dermatologists love this."*

### Relatability
Anchors in a shared, everyday scenario the audience immediately recognizes.
**Best for:** Unaware and Problem-Aware. Lifestyle and consumer products.
**Trigger:** Pain Agitation, Identity Call-Out
*Example: "When your coffee hits at 3pm..."*

### Reverse Psychology
Tells the viewer not to act to trigger reactance.
**Best for:** Ad-fatigued audiences. Contrarian personas.
**Trigger:** Pattern Interrupt, Curiosity Gap
*Example: "Don't click this."*

### Risk Reversal
Reduces the perceived risk of buying with guarantees or assurances.
**Best for:** Product-Aware audiences. High-ticket products.
**Trigger:** Social Proof/Credibility, Urgency/Stakes
*Example: "30-day money-back guarantee."*

### Shocking Statement
Leads with a surprising, provocative, or counter-intuitive claim.
**Best for:** Any awareness stage. Saturated feeds.
**Trigger:** Pattern Interrupt, Contrarian/Myth-Busting
*Example: "You've been doing this wrong your whole life..."*

### Social Proof
Leverages reviews, testimonials, or popularity signals to build trust.
**Best for:** Product-Aware audiences. Trust-barrier categories.
**Trigger:** Social Proof/Credibility
*Example: "10,000 5-star reviews." / "300,000 sold."*

### Statistic
Uses quantified evidence to establish credibility or impact.
**Best for:** Skeptical, analytical audiences. B2B and health.
**Trigger:** Social Proof/Credibility, Pattern Interrupt
*Example: "73% of people..." / "9 out of 10 users..."*

### Storytelling
Drops mid-moment into a personal or brand story.
**Best for:** Any awareness stage. Emotional products.
**Trigger:** Pain Agitation, Curiosity Gap, Social Proof/Credibility
*Example: "Three years ago I was broke..."*

### Urgency
Creates time or supply pressure to force a decision now.
**Best for:** Most-Aware audiences. Sale periods.
**Trigger:** Urgency/Stakes
*Example: "Ends tonight." / "Only 200 left."*

### Warning
Issues a sincere caution that halts the viewer's default behavior.
**Best for:** Problem-Aware audiences about to make a mistake.
**Trigger:** Pattern Interrupt, Pain Agitation
*Example: "Don't buy another pillow until you see this."*
